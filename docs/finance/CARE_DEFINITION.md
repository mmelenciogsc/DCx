# CARE distributable-net-profit definition

This is the implemented sandbox calculation, not legal, tax, employment, securities or accounting advice. Production requires counsel and accountant approval.

For each UTC calendar earning month, all inputs are nonnegative integer US-dollar cents:

`distributable net profit = max(0, recognized subscription revenue - refunds and chargebacks - PayPal/payment fees - approved operating costs - approved tax and risk reserve)`

The aggregate contributor pool is `floor(distributable net profit × configured basis points / 10,000)`. Configuration is rejected above 4,000 basis points, so the pool is at most 40% in aggregate—not 40% per contributor. Zero or negative defined net profit produces a zero pool.

The sandbox divides the pool by qualified usage weights after P10 fraud exclusions. Integer-cent rounding uses largest remainder with contributor pseudonym as the deterministic tie-break. Allocation sums exactly to the aggregate pool. Amounts below the configured threshold are held, not sent. Carry-forward, forfeiture and escheat treatment require legal/accounting policy before production.

The earning-period close is the month's final calendar date in UTC. Net 60 eligibility is exactly 60 calendar days after that date. Eligibility does not authorize payment: verified PayPal Payouts account capability, recipient eligibility, reconciliation, no active hold, and explicit owner approval are still required.

The official-contract-shaped sandbox adapter uses stable local/provider batch references. Every owner-approved item binds an immutable recipient-snapshot ID and digest of the exact destination; both the eligible directory record and provider receiver must match it. The local owner workflow protects the exact email in current-user Windows Credential Manager and persists only minimized binding metadata, but deliberately reports provider verification and eligibility as false. `PENDING`, `PROCESSING` and pending item states remain reserved. A terminal mixed outcome posts cash and clears payable only for successful items; failed amounts remain payable and never create a new batch automatically. A separately approved, ten-minute remediation review can reserve one exact target batch containing only selected unsuccessful source items, unchanged amounts/date and reviewed replacement snapshots. Immutable one-to-one source/target lineage prevents retrying an already remediated or successful item; missing lineage blocks restart and completion. A later lookup may move only a successful item once to `RETURNED`, `REFUNDED` or `REVERSED`, records the immutable correction and restores cash/payable atomically without resubmission. The accessible operator workflow, hold decisions, contributor notifications, production scheduling and credentialed provider acceptance still require separate approval and implementation.

Refunds, chargebacks and adjustments after period close require immutable correcting entries and re-reconciliation. Both fixture and durable paths now deny aggregate corrections above recorded subscription receipts. The verified-event durable path additionally binds each sale and caps its cumulative corrections at that sale's gross amount. It still does not implement production close/reopen or cross-period recovery.

The local sandbox reduces entitlement events by signed UTC occurrence time rather than arrival order. A newer event replaces current state; an older event is audited and any independently valid balanced financial correction is posted, but it cannot regress entitlement. At the same timestamp, deactivation dominates activation. Exact replay is idempotent, while reuse of an event or transmission identifier with different canonical content fails closed. Verified lifecycle events bind one configured plan to the provider subscription; payment requires that binding; sale corrections resolve through the recorded sale. If those dependencies have not arrived, schema v8 durably quarantines only the already verified normalized event and later re-evaluates it locally without another provider request. The server-side durable operations bridge defaults disabled and binds exact reviewed aggregate state plus explicit approval to bounded local replay under one repository-instance lock; it makes zero provider calls but provides neither authenticated transport nor cross-instance fencing. The Windows aggregate operations surface remains unavailable in normal mode; its generated-data fixture proves accessible review and one approved zero-provider-call cycle. This is still uncredentialed handler-backed evidence, not PayPal acceptance. Exact product/plan provisioning, merchant/Payouts and recipient eligibility, an approved provider-verification/retention policy for protected recipient snapshots, authenticated/authorized production transport with cross-instance fencing, scheduling/alerts, an approved accessible failed-item/quarantine disposition and contributor-notification workflow, credentialed remediation/later-return/quarantine acceptance, and accounting/legal approval remain required.
